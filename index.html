Tobacco, Bushfires, Health, and the Australian Government

From global consumption patterns to domestic environmental impacts and healthcare admissions, explore how tobacco shapes the Australian landscape across populations and geographic regions.

Global Smoking Prevalence

Australia has always had healthier smoking habits relative to the rest of the world. Even in the 2000s when Australia still produced their own tobacco, they were much healthier than most of the world. Australia's a well-developed country advocating for healthy lifestyle, and this statistic supports that fact especially when compared to less developed countries like India and Myanmar.

“Share of adults who smoke or use other tobacco,” Our World in Data
https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/share-of-adults-who-smoke (published 2024; accessed Oct. 20, 2026).

Tobacco Flows to Australia

Since 2006, commercial tobacco farming in Australia ceased entirely; all tobacco products are 100% imported. Stricter domestic regulations, advertising bans, and excise taxes coincided with domestic farming winding down while import flows shifted across global trade partners.

“UN comtrade,” United Nations
https://comtradeplus.un.org (published 2025, accessed Oct. 20, 2026).

Where Smokers Smoke

Smoking is most prominent in the developed, populated regions along Australia's eastern and southeastern coasts, where the majority of the population resides.

Multidimensional Health Impacts & Benchmarks

Simultaneously comparing attributable deaths, DALYs (Disability-Adjusted Life Years), and YLLs (Years of Life Lost) across global countries. Australia's profile stands out clearly with a high-contrast accent line, while dashed lines mark WHO regional benchmarks to evaluate holistic health outcomes. Australia ranks lower than most across the board, which is a good thing!

Australia vs. Global Tobacco Tax Stack Decomposition

Although the base price of cigarette packs across the world are similar, Australia's shelf price far exceeds the global average and that of select countries'! Even though final cost isn't everything, there is still a greater share of tax put on cigarette packs in Australia compared to the world average, suggesting that the Australian government is actively trying to dial down on smokers.
